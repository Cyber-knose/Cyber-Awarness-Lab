📧 Social Engineering Labs

Interactive educational modules to recognize and resist social engineering attacks

🎣 Phishing Email — General

A broad, mass-targeted email designed to trick recipients into clicking a malicious link or opening an infected attachment.

📨 From: security-alert@amaz0n-secure.example
⚠️ URGENT: Your Amazon account has been compromised!

Dear Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. Your account will be suspended within 24 hours if you do not verify your identity.

Click below to restore your account:

http://amaz0n-verify.example/login

(Hover the link — see it's not the real amazon.com)

🎯 Spear Phishing — Targeted Attack

A personalized attack targeting a specific individual using known details to appear authentic.

📨 From: jennifer.wilson@company-partners.example
Re: Q4 Budget Report — Action Required

Hi Sarah,

I'm following up on our meeting last Tuesday about the Q4 budget revisions. John from Finance mentioned you'd be handling the updates.

Attached is the revised budget template with notes from our CFO. Please review and sign before EOD.

📎 Q4_Budget_Rev2.docm (2.4 MB)

Note: The attacker researched the target on LinkedIn to craft this believable email.

📞 Vishing — Voice Phishing

Phone-based social engineering where attackers pose as legitimate organizations to extract sensitive information.

📞
⚠️ Incoming Call: "Microsoft Security Team"
Caller ID: +1 (800) 555-0192 (spoofed)

Call Transcript (Simulated):

Scammer: "Hello, this is David from Microsoft Windows Security. We've detected that your computer has been infected with 3 viruses. I'm calling to help you remove them before your data is stolen."

You: "What? I didn't notice anything wrong..."

Scammer: "That's because the malware is hidden. I need you to open the Event Viewer and check for error codes. If you give me remote access, I can fix this immediately."

... The scammer will then ask for credit card details to "activate the security package."

📷 QR Code Phishing (Quishing)

Attackers place malicious QR codes in public places or emails that redirect to phishing sites when scanned.

📱

SCAN ME FOR FREE WiFi

Warning! This QR code in a coffee shop actually redirects to:

http://free-wifi-login.example/credentials

Attackers print fake QR codes on stickers and place them over legitimate ones in cafes, airports, and parking meters.

🔐 Fake Login Portal

Attackers create perfect replicas of login pages to steal credentials when you click a phishing link.

📧

Outlook Web Access

🌐 https://outlook-verify.example.microsoft-login.com
⚠️ This is a FAKE login page! Your credentials would be stolen.
🔍 How to spot fake login portals:
  • Check the URL carefully — look for misspellings or extra subdomains
  • Real sites use HTTPS with valid certificates (click the padlock icon)
  • Never sign in from an email link — type the URL manually
  • Use a password manager — it won't autofill on fake sites

🛠️ Fake Tech Support Scam

Scammers pose as tech support from Microsoft, Apple, or your ISP to gain remote access to your computer.

⚠️

Your PC May Be Infected!

Windows Defender Alert: 3 critical threats detected

🔴 Trojan.Generic.284732
🔴 Spyware.Agent.9912
🔴 Rootkit.Boot.445

Call Windows Support immediately: +1-800-555-0199

This is a FAKE popup — real Windows Defender never shows phone numbers.

💾 USB Drop Attack Simulation

Attackers leave infected USB drives in parking lots, lobbies, and common areas — waiting for a curious person to plug them in.

💾 "EMPLOYEE BONUSES_Q4.xlsx"

Click the USB drive to see what happens...

⚠️ You just plugged in a malicious USB device! The BadUSB automatically:

✅ Emulated a keyboard (HID attack)
✅ Opened a reverse shell in 3 seconds
✅ Installed persistent backdoor
✅ Exfiltrated documents to attacker's server

This is a common social engineering tactic used in penetration tests.
🛡️ Never plug in unknown USB drives!
  • 🔌 USB devices can emulate keyboards — typing commands automatically
  • 🔌 Use USB data blockers (USB condoms) for charging only
  • 🔌 Disable AutoRun on Windows to prevent automatic execution
  • 🔌 Report found USB drives to IT security — don't plug them in

Attackers create fake accounts impersonating trusted people or brands to gather intelligence or trick connections.

👤
Sarah Connors
HR Director — Acme Corp
896 connections
✅ REAL PROFILE
👤
Sarah Connors
HR Director — Acme Corp
2 connections
🚩 FAKE — Impersonator

Scenario: The fake profile sends a connection request to employees with a message:

"Hi! I'm updating our employee directory. Could you confirm your full name, department, and personal email for our records? Thanks!"

The attacker collects data for a targeted phishing campaign against the company.
